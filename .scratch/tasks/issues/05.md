# 05: A renewal is not a change

**What to build:** a renewal of a claim moves the task's lease, and nothing else. It does not change when the task last changed, so it does not move the task in the lens's order or in its Updated column. Since Sync 05 every `task.renewed` reaches the replica and counts as the task's latest change, so a task whose agent renews rises above the tasks that really changed.

**Blocked by:** 02, Sync 05

**Status:** ready-for-agent

- [x] A task's last change is its latest task event other than a renewal; a renewal still moves the claim's `until` and the lease bar.
- [x] Tests come first, in the core: a renewal after a claim keeps the task's place and its last change, and moves its `until`.
- [x] Verified by hand on `vb serve` with an agent renewing a claim: the task's row keeps its place and its Updated time while its lease bar refills.

## Comments

Built on 2026-11-23 by a subagent in its own worktree, test-first, on main at cb7e811. The core gained 4 tests, 2 in the replica's suite and 2 in the Tasks reader's, and all four failed first because each renewal moved its task's last change to the renewal's own time. One is for a root that arrives after its claim and renewal, as a copy can deliver them. Another is for a reader opened after the renewal, which reads the order from the tables.

**What was built.**
- **A task's last change is its latest task event other than a renewal.** The replica's `tasks.latest_event_at` became `changed_at`, and storing a `task.renewed` no longer moves it. A root that arrives after its events takes the latest of them that is not a renewal.
- **A renewal still does everything else.** It stays among its task's events, and the root's view it carries still moves the claim's `until`. Since that updates the task's row, it logs a task change, and the reader catches up. The task goes back to the place it had, and the shell announces a `TaskChange`. The lens then reads its window again, so the lease bar refills. The lease is still measured from the latest `task.claimed` or `task.renewed`, as Tasks 02 decided.
- **The reader and the UI needed nothing else.** The order reads `changed_at`, falling back to the root's time as before.
- **The schema is version 8,** so a replica from before is copied again.

Decisions made along the way:
- **The column was renamed, not just given a new meaning.** It no longer holds the latest event, and `latest_event_at` would say it does. The new name follows the contract's `changedAt`, which the lens's Updated column shows.
- **Moving the schema version is what corrects the replicas made since Sync 05.** Without a new copy, their tasks would keep a renewal's time as their last change until each one changed again.
- **Only `task.renewed` is not a change.** It is the one task event Board itself counts as bookkeeping, shown only with `all=true`. Every other task event changes something the lens or the thread shows: the state, who holds the task, whom it is for, or what it waits for.

**Verified by hand** against `vb serve` 0.54.0 on port 4600. The release build ran under a home of its own. A temporary harness in the page, not in this change, chose Tasks and read the lens's rows every 2 seconds: their order, the Updated cell with its time, and the lease's meter.
- **The board:** six tasks in `#work`, made with `vb tasks create`. `codex` claimed task 6, "fix CVE-123 in acme/web", on a one-minute lease. `claude` claimed task 4 and `gemini` task 10 on ten-minute leases, 4 seconds apart, so task 6 changed first and sits last in Claimed. `codex` then renewed every 15 seconds with `vb tasks renew 6 --as codex --lease 1m`.
- **The first copy,** at load 5.4, made after two renewals: Claimed read 10, 4, 6. Task 6's Updated was its claim at 21:45:30, though it had been renewed at 21:45:39 and 21:46:00, and its lease read 48 of 60 s, measured from the second renewal.
- **The first run's follow was cut short.** The machine slept several times from 21:46, and `vb serve` expired all three claims as it woke. The second run held the machine awake with `caffeinate -i` and claimed the three tasks again while the app followed.
- **While followed,** across 10 renewals from 22:38:51 to 22:41:08, at loads of 15.7 falling to 4.1: Claimed read 10, 4, 6 in all 73 readings. Task 6's Updated stayed at its claim, 22:38:33, aging from "17 s" to "2 min". Its lease counted down from 60 s, and the first reading to show it refilled came 1.1 to 3.1 s after each renewal, with readings 2 s apart. Sync 05 measured 1.1 to 2.1 s for a new `until` to reach the replica.
- **In the replica,** read with the app closed: task 6 held 13 renewals among its events, 3 from the first run and 10 from this one. Its `changed_at` was its latest claim, 22:38:33.216.
- **Opened again on the kept replica,** at load 5.8, while `codex` kept renewing: Claimed read 10, 4, 6 in all 31 readings. Task 6's Updated stayed at 22:38:33, aging from "3 min" to "4 min". The renewal made while the app was closed arrived through the feed. The next 5 refilled the lease in the first reading 0.8 to 2.6 s after each one.

Left for later:
- **Each renewal still adds a row to `task_changes`,** because it updates the task's row with the new `until`. Sync 06 trims that log (ADR 0024).

Seen along the way, for a later ticket: 2 of the 120 readings the first app made found no row of the lens on screen at all. They came at 22:38:49 and 22:38:57, 8 s apart, in the first minute after the machine woke, at load near 15. The windows keep their rows when they read again, so the list itself must have left the page for a moment. One way that can happen is the open board reading "none running" and then "open" again, which unmounts the lens. The app logged no error. The reopened app showed none in 31 readings, and this change does not touch that path.
