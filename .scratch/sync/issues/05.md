# 05: Renewals reach the replica

**What to build:** the copy and the live follow read Board's feed with `all=true`, so the bookkeeping events reach the replica: `task.renewed` keeps each claim's `until` current, and `actor.seen` keeps who's who. Today the feed hides them, so a claim keeps its first `until`, and on Vercel's hosted board, where a lease lasts at most five minutes, an agent that renews reads as lapsed after its first lease (found in Fleet 01).

**Blocked by:** Flow 07, which changes the copy

**Status:** ready-for-agent

- [x] The copy and the follow ask for `all=true`; a renewal stores the root's view it carries, and the task's `until` follows it.
- [x] Bookkeeping events that concern no message (`thread.synced`, `message.synced`, `message.files_synced`) are read and skipped without error; `actor.seen` keeps the actors table.
- [x] Tests come first, against the fake board: a renewal after a claim moves its `until`, and the fleet stops reading the agent as lapsed.
- [x] Verified by hand on `vb serve`: an agent that renews its claim keeps its lease in the replica, and what `all` adds to the copy of the 100,000-message board is measured.

## Comments

Built on 2026-11-21 by a subagent in its own worktree, test-first, on `main` at c1f687b. The core gained 5 tests, 4 in sync and 1 in the fleet, and two sync tests were moved onto the fake board's hidden events.

**What was built.**

- **The copy's replay and the follow ask for `all=true`** (`protocol::WITH_BOOKKEEPING`). The stream adds it to its URL, built the way `listed_actors` builds its own, since reqwest here is compiled without its `query` feature.
- **A renewal needed nothing new to be stored.** `vb serve` 0.54.0 sends `task.renewed` with the root's view after the renewal: a higher `version` and the new `claim.until`. The batch already stores the newest view and keeps every task event, so the task's `until` follows, and the renewal joins its task's events, where Tasks 02 already measures a lease from the latest `task.claimed` or `task.renewed`.
- **`actor.seen` keeps the actors table.** It carries no message and no top-level `actor`; the actor's details are its payload (`actor`, `kind`, `handle`, `name`, and `via` for an agent), which the batch now reads into the actors it keeps, the latest last.
- **Bookkeeping that concerns no message is read and skipped.** `thread.synced`, `message.synced` and `message.files_synced` hold nothing the replica keeps; they pass through the batch without error, and the feed's cursor moves past them.
- **The fake board serves bookkeeping only to a feed that asks for `all=true`,** as Board does: renewals, `actor.seen` and a thread's sync receipts. What it called bookkeeping before, an event the stream shows only by its ID, is now a hidden event, one the feed does not show this caller.

Decisions made along the way:

- **Every event must carry its `payload`.** Board always sends one, and an event without it is one the core cannot read, which stops the replica and says so, as any unreadable event does.
- **The test that bookkeeping is skipped could not fail first.** Until the feed asks for `all=true`, Board and the fake board never send those events. It guards the change instead: with `all=true`, an event the batch could not read would stop the replica.

**Verified by hand** on a `vb serve` 0.54.0 of the subagent's own, on port 4300 with its log in the scratch folder. The release build ran under a home of its own, with a temporary harness in the page that subscribed to the fleet and reported each change. It ran against the same build with `all=false`, Board's default, in place of `all=true`. Each run created a task, claimed it as `claude` on a 30 s lease, renewed it three times 20 s apart, then stopped renewing:

| | Without bookkeeping (load 3.04) | With bookkeeping (load 1.55) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The replica's `until` after each renewal | stayed at the first lease's end | followed it, within the 2 s before each look |
| The task's events | created, claimed, expired | created, claimed, renewed three times, expired |
| The fleet while the agent renewed | lapsed 0.6 s after the first lease ended, and for the next 60 s while it held the claim | holding, never lapsed; each new `until` arrived 1.1 to 2.1 s after the renewal |
| Once it stopped renewing | lapsed, through `vb serve`'s `task.expired` | lapsed by lease for 24 ms, then through `task.expired` |

- **What `vb serve` timing its own leases means locally.** A lease that runs out ends with a `task.expired` the board writes itself, a fraction of a second later. So on a local board, "lapsed" can come from a passed lease only for that moment, and from an expiry otherwise. The false "lapsed" of an agent that renews shows locally only while renewals do not reach the replica, as in the first column. On Vercel's board, which writes no expiry and lets a lease last at most five minutes, it is the only way the fleet sees a lapse; the fake board's tests cover that case.
- **The copy keeps renewals too:** copying the same board again from an empty folder (load 2.25) recorded every renewal on it, the three of the run without bookkeeping among them.

**What `all=true` adds to the first copy of the 100,000-message board** (100,070 messages, 40 agent profiles, no tasks):

- Replayed from the oldest root by a script, as the copy replays it, alternately with and without `all=true`. It adds 40 events, all `actor.seen`, one per agent profile, and no request: 502 either way. It adds 10,442 bytes to 139,953,554 (0.007%, loads 3.96 and 4.85). The replay took 6.36 s without and 6.64 s with (loads 2.70 and 2.51), 6.92 s and 6.88 s (3.43 and 3.23), and 6.38 s and 6.36 s (3.20 and 3.10).
- The app's whole copy, from an empty replicas folder, alternately:

| Round | Without bookkeeping | Load | With bookkeeping | Load |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 15.74 s | 1.42 | 15.76 s | 2.02 |
| 2 | 15.81 s | 2.16 | 15.89 s | 2.43 |
| 3 | 15.89 s | 2.48 | 15.91 s | 2.50 |

- The 0.02 to 0.09 s difference is within noise. Both ended with 41 actors, so `actor.seen` named no one the messages' authors had not.

Left for later:

- **Each renewal moves its task's latest change,** as every task event does. In the Tasks lens, a task whose agent renews reads as just updated and rises within Claimed. That fits "last change"; whether a renewal should count as one there is the Tasks lens's decision.
- **Vercel's board shows `actor.seen` only to a feed without filters,** read by a caller who reads every channel. A copy made one channel at a time, as the hosted board needs, would not get it, and would keep learning actors from messages' authors and from `/actors`, as it does now.
