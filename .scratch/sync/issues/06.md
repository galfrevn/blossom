# 06: The replica's logs of changes stay small

**What to build:** the writer deletes the rows of `task_changes` and `message_changes` that every open reader has read, as ADR 0024 decides. `arrivals` is kept whole. Today both logs grow for as long as the replica lives, and since Sync 05 every renewal of a claim adds to `task_changes`.

**Blocked by:** Flow 08, Sync 05

**Status:** ready-for-agent

- [x] A reader tells the writer how far it has read in each log, and the writer deletes up to the lowest point among the open readers, never inside a copy's batch and never while no reader is open.
- [x] No reader misses a change: a change written while a reader catches up is read on its next catch-up.
- [x] Tests come first, against a real SQLite replica: rows read are deleted, rows not read by every reader are kept, and a reader that opens after a trim builds the same Flow and Tasks as one that never trimmed.
- [x] Measured on `vb serve` with an agent renewing a claim every few seconds for ten minutes: the two logs stay at a handful of rows, and what a trim costs is written in the comments.

## Comments

Built on 2026-11-23 by a subagent in its own worktree, test-first, on main at 6a4a20d. The core gained 7 tests in `core/tests/logs.rs`, against real SQLite replicas.
- **Five failed first, because nothing was deleted:** rows every reader has read, rows only one of two readers has read, a change stored after every row was deleted, a change stored as the writer deletes, and a reader that opens after a trim.
- **Two passed from the start and guard what must not happen:** nothing is deleted while no reader is open, or by a copy's batch.
- **With the deletes wired in, two failed again, as they should.** A log emptied by a trim gave its next row sequence 1, below what the reader had read, so the reader skipped it. That is why the logs now use `AUTOINCREMENT`.

**What was built.**
- **A reader tells the writer how far it has read.** `core/src/replica/logs.rs` keeps, for the process, how far each open reader has read in `task_changes` and in `message_changes`.
  - A reader joins as it opens, before its first read, as having read nothing, so the writer deletes nothing until it says otherwise.
  - It says how far it read once it has built itself, and after each `catch_up` and `catch_up_tasks`.
  - Dropping it takes it off the list.
- **The writer deletes in `apply_from_feed`,** in the follow's own transaction. It deletes the rows at or below the lowest point among the open readers of that replica, and nothing while none is open. `apply`, which the copy uses, deletes nothing.
- **The two logs number their rows with `AUTOINCREMENT`,** so a sequence is never used twice. `arrivals` is untouched.
- **The schema is version 9,** so a replica from before is copied again.
- **The shell needed no change:** it already drops its reader when it lets go of a replica, and catches it up after every batch.
- **ADR 0024 gained three consequences:** `AUTOINCREMENT` and why, how readers tell the writer, and that only the follow's batches trim.

Decisions made along the way:
- **The list lives in memory, not in a table.** The reader's connection is read-only. A row in a table would also outlive a reader that crashed and hold the logs back forever, while the list empties when the process ends, which is "nothing while no reader is open".
- **The replica is named by SQLite's own name for its file** (`Connection::path`), so the reader and the writer agree without either keeping a path. Keeping one in `Replica` made `KnownReplicaOpening` in `keeping.rs` large enough for clippy's `large_enum_variant`.
- **A reader joins before its first read, as having read nothing.** If it joined after its first read, another reader could let the writer delete rows written between the opening reader's first read and its first report, and the opening reader would never see them. The shell runs one reader at a time, but the list does not rely on that.
- **`AUTOINCREMENT`, not "never delete the newest row".** It is SQLite's own guarantee, stated in the schema, where the other would have been an unexplained condition in a `DELETE`. It costs nothing measurable, below.
- **The trim runs with the follow's batches, not on a timer.** The rows a reader has read wait for the next batch: on a busy board a second or two, on a quiet one until the board next says anything.

**Measured** against `vb serve` 0.54.0 on port 4600, on the 10,000-task board of Tasks 06 (10,002 messages). The release build ran under a home of its own, with temporary timing in the trim that printed the rows deleted and the microseconds taken; it is not in this change. The two logs were counted from outside every 15 seconds with `sqlite3`.
- **The copy:** the app found its schema 8 replica, discarded it and copied the board again in 3 s. The copy left 24,193 rows in `task_changes` and none in `message_changes`, and deleted nothing.
- **The agent:** `codex` claimed a task in `fleet/area5` on a 60 s lease, renewed it every 5 seconds for ten minutes, 120 renewals, then released it. Each of the follow's 122 batches trimmed.
- **The first trim after the copy** deleted the copy's 24,193 rows in 3.2 ms, at load 7.4.
- **Every trim after it** deleted 0 to 2 rows: 6 µs at the median, 12 µs at the 95th percentile and 59 µs at worst, at loads of 3.2 to 7.4.
- **The logs stayed at a handful of rows.** After the first trim, `task_changes` held exactly 1 row in all 40 samples: the latest renewal's, which the reader read after its batch and the next batch deleted. `message_changes` held none. The log's sequence went from 24,193 to 24,314: 121 rows written and deleted.
- **What `AUTOINCREMENT` costs:** 100,000 rows into a fresh log with the `sqlite3` CLI took 24 to 27 ms both with and without it, in five rounds alternating the two, at load 5.7.

Left for later:
- **On a board that goes quiet,** the rows read since the last batch stay until the next one: at most what one batch logged.
- **Making a large board's threads into tasks at once,** as Flow 08 did with 93,951 replies, now leaves those rows only until the follow's next batch. Its trim was not timed here; the copy's 24,193 rows took 3.2 ms.
